# One-piece illustrated itinerary handbook

Use for a finished, illustrated itinerary image that the traveler can read and check on a phone, save offline, or print. Keep the editable source plan and evidence separate; the image is a dated field reference, not a live booking record.

## Visual direction

The Moalboal-style reference is a portrait editorial travel infographic, not a photograph collage or decorative poster with tiny itinerary text:

- A large destination headline, a compact date/duration ribbon, and a visible local-time/status line at the top.
- A white or warm-white ground, deep navy headings, teal section bars, pale aqua time cells, and restrained yellow emphasis for an important transport or deadline detail. Adapt the illustrations and accent palette to the destination; do not apply tropical beaches to every trip.
- Rounded day panels arranged in chronological reading order. Each row has a simple transport/activity icon and time or time range at left, then a bold action title and concise practical detail. Align these columns consistently.
- A small number of lively, clean illustrated vignettes: transport, destination scenery and the signature activity. Keep them at the edges or in intentional open space, never behind small text or over route instructions.
- Bottom panels for “Before you go” and a realistic fallback if conditions change; a quiet footer with revision date and short source/check-date notes.

Use roughly a 2:3 portrait canvas for a short weekend by default, adjusting height for readable content. A long trip may need a taller single image or a concise overview plus a separately requested detailed edition. Do not shrink essential type to force every note into one sheet. Match the user's language; do not silently translate proper names or generate bilingual duplication unless useful or requested.

## Establish the content before drawing

Read the current itinerary and latest user corrections. Inspect an available reference image's actual pixels when matching it; do not claim a visual match from a text description alone. If it is inaccessible, explain the limitation and use the design direction above rather than pretending to have viewed it.

Prepare a compact text specification from the canonical plan, with:

- Trip dates, weekdays, duration, local timezone, overnight coverage and hard return deadlines.
- Each leg's boarding/arrival point, direction or route qualifier, travel time, transfers, waiting/traffic buffer and last-mile arrangements.
- Hotel name, stay dates and status. Include check-in/out and relevant cancellation restrictions when useful; include prices only if requested or part of the established handbook. Omit booking codes, contact details and other unnecessary personal data.
- Activities, meal/rest windows, prerequisites, pending arrangements, essential packing and a safe fallback.

Assign facts their real status: user decision, confirmed booking, proposed target, estimate, or unverified. A confirmed hotel does not confirm its associated tour, school permission or transfer. Separate an approximate service frequency from an actual timetable and from a target boarding time. Preserve “around,” ranges and uncertainty instead of inventing precision. For past-trip records, distinguish visited stops from a reference route and do not assume every pictured attraction was entered.

Research volatile facts when needed, preferably from current primary sources. Keep full source links and verification dates in the accompanying text or canonical page; use short source labels in the footer. A new image generation date is not a fresh fact-check date. Never carry another traveler's old dates, prices, suppliers or school rules into a reusable template.

Check the time arithmetic forward and backward, including checkout, luggage, transport waits and hard deadlines. Resolve conflicting evidence or visibly leave the specific issue pending before rendering.

## Generate and inspect

Use the available image-generation/editing capability and its current instructions for raster artwork. Supply the reference image when available, the visual direction, and the exact approved text specification. Request a complete high-resolution image with readable typography, generous spacing and no invented facts. For an existing raster image, use the supported image-editing workflow; preserve unrelated layout and content. Existing code-native SVG assets remain governed by the route-diagram workflow rather than being silently rasterized.

If image generation is unavailable, state that limitation and offer an editable vector/HTML export only as an alternative, not as a supposedly generated illustration. Do not claim a finished image when only a prompt exists.

Inspect the final pixels at full size and a phone-width preview, not just the prompt or OCR. Check:

1. Every date, amount, place name, route direction, time and booking label against the text specification.
2. Text completeness, spelling, glyphs, contrast and hierarchy; no clipped lines, crowded cells or illustrations covering instructions.
3. Legible critical actions on a phone and readable secondary detail when zoomed. For a requested printed edition, inspect the target print size as well.
4. Consistency between status note, hotel row, checklist, caption and footer. A changed booking must not remain “unconfirmed” in another panel.
5. Safe buffers and fallback instructions remain visible rather than being removed to make room for decoration.

Correct discrepancies and inspect the final revision again. OCR can assist but does not replace visual review. Report any remaining verification limit honestly. Save the image and its text specification through the user's established artifact workflow so later changes can be made without re-creating the facts from memory.

## Deliver and revise

Deliver the actual image in the requested channel. Upload or embed it in an existing Notion itinerary only within the user's authorization; preserve unrelated notes. Verify each attachment/update succeeded and distinguish local creation from remote publication. Keep a text companion or canonical-page link for accessibility and usable source links.

For daily actual-itinerary pages in Notion, place the current illustrated image directly at the top, outside any toggle. Put the detailed chronological account and supporting notes in a clearly labelled native toggle below it, preserving all detail rather than deleting it. Keep the image readable without expansion, and verify that the toggle opens and retains the complete text. This image-first preference does not require hiding current decisions or urgent safety/status caveats on planning pages.

On revision, preserve the visual identity and change the affected facts, dependent panels and revision date. Reconcile image, caption and canonical itinerary; mark retained older exports clearly as superseded instead of leaving contradictory images equally current. Do not change reservations, send supplier messages or publish personal trip details to a public repository merely because a handbook was requested.

For skill maintenance, record reusable design and validation guidance, not the user's private itinerary or booking image. A historical visual can inform the style without being committed to a public repo.
